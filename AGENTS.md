# Agent Guidance: Inclusive Media Interfaces

## Purpose and authority

Use this repository for design, research, and review of audiovisual interfaces and physical or connected controls that support media access. Follow the README and keep interface design distinct from content production, caption authoring, general modality principles, and legal assessment.

An agent may summarize and apply guidance. It must not claim a device, platform, programme, service, or operator is legally compliant without appropriate evidence and human authority.

## Required context

Before making a specific recommendation, identify or request:

- the service and interface type;
- device, player, remote, connected product, and software version where known;
- task and user goal;
- viewing or listening environment and relevant constraints;
- access services available and the media version;
- whether the question concerns interface discovery/control, physical hardware, environmental control, content production, caption authoring, or regulatory evidence.

Do not invent missing details. Label assumptions and identify what needs to be checked.

## Review method

For a relevant journey, consider:

1. Discovery: Can the person find content, access services, and settings?
2. Setup: Can they configure the device without an unavailable method or unexplained sequence?
3. Control: Can they operate playback, navigation, connected products, and relevant access services using suitable input methods?
4. State: Is focus, selection, playback, connected-system state, and consequence clear?
5. Continuity: Do task context and preferences behave as expected across content, sessions, and devices? Do not assume persistence without user control.
6. Recovery: Can the person pause, resume, correct, exit, and recover after error or interruption?
7. Environment: Consider distance, lighting, noise, fatigue, time pressure, and shared use without assuming the same effect for everyone.
8. Ecosystem: Check interoperability, feedback, maintenance, privacy, and security where products are connected.
9. Evidence: Distinguish source guidance, user evidence, technical inspection, inference, and untested recommendations.

## Evidence and maintenance

- Read relevant source files and retain their provenance.
- Do not treat synthetic personas, automated scans, simulated tasks, or agent reasoning as research with disabled people.
- Preserve negative, minority, and contradictory evidence; do not average away serious barriers.
- Separate access-service availability from discoverability, usability, and successful activation.
- Keep one canonical version of each substantive guide. Update source migration links only after the destination has been reviewed.
- State uncertainty, evidence limits, and what could change the recommendation.
